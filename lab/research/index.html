Note 001 · 23 November 2026

Does observed weather improve weekly storage estimates?

Across 463 historical evaluation weeks, a model using observed heating and cooling degree-day deviations reduced mean absolute error from 30.50 Bcf to 20.50 Bcf relative to a five-year seasonal median. Both models use the same evaluation dates, from January 2018 through November 2026.

Method

The benchmark uses the median of five prior seasonal analogs. A rolling regression adjusts it for deviations in observed CONUS heating and cooling degree days, with training restricted to earlier weeks. Data come from EIA and NOAA CPC.

Limitations

This is a retrospective replay with current-vintage historical observations, not verified live forecasting performance. Original historical publication times are not established for every observation. No completed live forecasts had been scored at the snapshot date. Annual results are descriptive and 2026 is partial.

Note 002 · 23 November 2026

One benchmark, different delivery months

Two futures-curve snapshots reconstructed from SEC filings show January 2027 delivery falling from $4.391 per MMBtu on 30 June 2026 to $3.752 on 30 September 2026, a decline of $0.639 per MMBtu. Other overlapping delivery months changed by different amounts.

Matching the same delivery month helps distinguish calendar-curve repricing from changes caused by rolling a front-month series. These two snapshots establish differences in quoted prices; they do not identify which fundamental factor caused them.
